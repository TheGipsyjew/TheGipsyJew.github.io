# How to analyze a competitor’s Amazon reviews

By TheGipsyjew · Published and checked on 13 November 2026 · [Editorial method](https://thegipsyjew.github.io/reviewpluck/method/)

The short answer

Export all reviews of the competing product, start with the 1 and 2-star ones, tag each with the problem it describes, count the tags, then compare with the 4 and 5-star reviews to see what customers value. The themes that keep coming back are your product brief and your listing’s selling points.

## 1. Export every review

A sample of the first page is biased toward the reviews Amazon ranks as most helpful. Export the full list (see [how to export Amazon reviews to CSV](https://thegipsyjew.github.io/reviewpluck/export-amazon-reviews/)) and keep one file per product and per marketplace: customers in Germany and in the US do not always complain about the same things.

## 2. Read the low ratings first

Filter the note column on 1 and 2 stars. These reviews are shorter to read and richer in concrete defects. With ReviewPluck Pro you can filter before exporting, by rating, period, verified purchase, variant or keyword.

## 3. Tag recurring problems

Add a theme column and give each review one or two short tags: leaks, size runs small, battery, arrived damaged. Keep the list of tags short; merge synonyms as you go. Separate product defects from delivery issues, which you do not control the same way.

## 4. Count and rank the tags

A pivot table on the theme column gives the frequency of each problem. Add the variante column to see whether a defect concerns one size or colour only, and the date column to check whether it is recent or was fixed.

## 5. Compare with the high ratings

Do the same on 4 and 5-star reviews: what do satisfied customers mention first? Those words are the benefits to put forward in your own title, bullet points and images.

## 6. Turn it into decisions

- Product: the top three defects become the specification of your version.

- Listing: answer the most frequent objection in the first bullet point.

- Support: anticipate the questions behind the complaints in your instructions or FAQ.

Keep the files: a new extraction a few months later shows whether the competitor fixed the issue.
